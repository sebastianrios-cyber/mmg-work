# Niche Finding

The single biggest mistake new creators make: choosing a niche based on what they're interested in, not whether the niche can become a business. Interest keeps you going. Product-market fit makes you money.

## The Product-Market Fit Framework

A viable YouTube niche has three components. If any one is missing, the other two don't save you.

---

### Component 1: Problem Depth

Your channel must be about a problem that is genuinely expensive - not mildly inconvenient.

Expensive means costly in at least one of three ways:
- **Money** - costs $500–$5,000+ per month if unsolved
- **Time** - consumes 10+ hours per week
- **Emotional pain** - keeps people up at night, creates anxiety, affects identity

**Why this matters:** When your free YouTube video helps someone make real progress on an expensive problem, the trust you build is enormous. That trust converts to revenue. Views alone never do.

**How to find deep problems:**
Go to Reddit, Facebook Groups, Quora, and niche forums. Search for your potential topic. Look for these exact phrases:
- "I've tried everything"
- "I'm so frustrated with this"
- "I spent thousands and nothing works"
- "I don't know what else to do"

Those phrases = gold. That's your niche's problem depth confirmed.

**Save that language.** You'll use it word-for-word in your titles and thumbnails later.

---

### Component 2: Market Willingness

A painful problem in a market that never pays for solutions = dead end.

You need to verify your audience actually buys things. Check:

1. **Other channels in the niche** - Do they sell courses, software, paid communities? Do they have sponsors paying to reach this audience?
2. **Blogs in the niche** - Do they run affiliate links, promote products, sell memberships?
3. **Reddit/forums** - Are people asking for product recommendations, paying for coaches, buying tools?

If the answer is yes across at least two of these: market willingness confirmed.

If you find a deep problem but nobody in the niche sells anything - the audience consumes free content only. Hard pass.

---

### Component 3: Solution Proximity

This is what separates channels making $500/month from channels making $50,000/month.

Solution proximity = how direct the line is between what your videos teach and what you eventually sell.

**Good (straight line):**
- Videos about YouTube growth → sell a YouTube growth course
- Videos about dog training → sell a dog training program
- Videos about personal finance → sell a budgeting tool or financial coaching

**Bad (broken line):**
- Videos about YouTube growth → sell an email marketing course
- Videos about fitness → sell a business productivity app

The more direct the line, the higher your conversion rate. Every degree of separation cuts your revenue.

## Niche Scoring Worksheet

Score each component 1–3. Total of 7+ = viable niche. Below 6 = rethink.

| Component | Score (1–3) | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| Problem Depth | | Costly in money, time, or emotion? |
| Market Willingness | | Do they buy solutions? |
| Solution Proximity | | Clear line to monetization? |
| **Total** | **/9** | |

**Scoring guide:**
- 3 = Strong signal confirmed with research
- 2 = Moderate signal, some evidence
- 1 = Weak, mostly assumption

---

## Common Niche Mistakes

**Too broad:** "my channel is about fitness" - no specific problem, no specific buyer
**Too shallow:** life hacks, general entertainment - lots of views, almost no revenue
**No market willingness:** art tutorials, poetry - deep passion niche but rarely buys
**Solution too far from content:** educational content in niche A, selling product in niche B

---

## Research Process (30-minute niche audit)

1. Pick 2–3 niche candidates
2. Spend 10 min on Reddit/forums per niche - look for deep-problem language
3. Find 3 channels per niche - check what they sell or promote
4. Score each niche on the worksheet
5. Pick the highest scorer that you can speak to credibly

Credibility doesn't mean you're an expert. It means you're willing to learn out loud, document your journey, or interview people who are experts.
