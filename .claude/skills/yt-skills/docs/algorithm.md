# Algorithm

YouTube's recommendation system has fundamentally changed. Understanding the current state - not how it worked 3 years ago - determines whether your content gets pushed or buried.

---

## The Biggest Shift: Subscribers Don't Matter

YouTube no longer primarily pushes content to subscribers. It pushes content to people based on **interest**, regardless of whether they've heard of you.

YouTube's own words: "Audiences are initially drawn to creators through specific shared interests."

What this means practically:
- A brand new channel with zero subscribers can get pushed to thousands of relevant viewers within days
- A channel with 100,000 subscribers gets no preferential treatment over a channel with 100
- The playing field is more level now than at any point in YouTube's history

Your only job: make content that performs well when YouTube tests it on a small audience. If it performs, YouTube scales it. If it doesn't, it doesn't.

---

## How YouTube Actually Recommends Content

### Step 1: The Test
When you upload a video, YouTube shows it to a small test audience (a few hundred people). This audience is selected based on:
- What topics they've watched recently
- What channels they engage with
- Viewer history and interest graph

### Step 2: The Performance Signals
YouTube measures how that test audience responds:

| Signal | What it measures |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Click-through rate (CTR)** | Did they click the thumbnail/title? |
| **Average view duration** | How much of the video did they watch? |
| **Session time** | Did they keep watching YouTube after your video? |
| **Viewer investment signals** | Did they click links, search related topics, come back to your channel? |

### Step 3: The Scale Decision
If test performance exceeds thresholds: YouTube pushes the video to a larger audience. That audience's performance determines the next expansion.

If test performance is poor: the video gets minimal distribution. More uploads won't fix a structural problem.

## Viewer Investment Signals

This is the metric almost nobody optimizes for - and it's increasingly central to how YouTube ranks content.

Viewer investment signals include:
- Clicking links in your video description
- Searching for related topics after watching your video
- Returning to your channel to watch more videos
- Watching multiple videos in one session

**Why YouTube cares:** These behaviors indicate the viewer got genuine value. They're engaged with the topic, not just passively watching. YouTube wants to surface content that generates real engagement, not just passive consumption.

**How to trigger them:**
- Mention related resources in-video that viewers will search for
- Create content series where one video naturally leads to the next
- Reference your pillar video as the logical "next step" (and it delivers on that promise)
- Use end screens to extend sessions (see [channel-structure.md](./channel-structure.md))

---

## Session Time

Session time = total time a viewer spends on YouTube after watching your video.

YouTube's incentive: keep people on YouTube as long as possible. Content that consistently leads to long sessions gets more distribution.

**Flywheel structure optimizes for this directly:**
- 10-minute regular video → end screen → 45-minute pillar video = 55 minutes of session time from one viewer

This is why the flywheel structure benefits from algorithmic promotion beyond just the conversion math.

---

## Why Small Channels Can Blow Up Now

YouTube has a dedicated team specifically tasked with finding small channels and pushing them to large audiences. This is confirmed, not rumored.

Why YouTube does this: more successful creators = more content = more time on platform = more ad revenue. YouTube has a direct financial incentive to make small creators succeed if their content performs.

This creates a genuine window. A channel with 50 subscribers showing strong test performance can be pushed to millions of viewers. But the window is time-limited - as more creators understand this, the bar for "strong test performance" rises.

---

## What Gets a Video Buried

- **High CTR + low watch time:** clickbait. YouTube penalizes this heavily. You get the click; viewer leaves in 30 seconds; YouTube stops pushing it.
- **Low CTR + high watch time:** your content is good but your title/thumbnail isn't working. Fix the packaging.
- **Low CTR + low watch time:** topic, packaging, and content all need work.
- **Low session time:** viewers watch your video and leave YouTube. Either your content doesn't lead naturally to more content, or it satisfies the query completely without generating curiosity.

## Algorithm Checklist

**Before uploading:**
- [ ] Title and thumbnail optimized for CTR (see [titles-thumbnails.md](./titles-thumbnails.md))
- [ ] Video uses tension engine to maximize watch time (see [script-writing.md](./script-writing.md))
- [ ] End screen configured to extend session (points to pillar video)

**After uploading (first 48 hours):**
- [ ] Check CTR in YouTube Studio → Reach → Impressions click-through rate
- [ ] Check average view duration in YouTube Studio → Engagement
- [ ] If CTR < 3% on browse: test new thumbnail or title
- [ ] If average view duration < 40%: hook or tension engine needs work

**Channel-level:**
- [ ] All videos share a consistent niche (YouTube's interest graph needs clear signal)
- [ ] Pillar video exists and end screens are set
- [ ] Upload consistency: at minimum 1 video/week (consistency trains the algorithm to expect and distribute your content)

---

## What Consistency Actually Means

Consistency doesn't mean daily uploads. It means predictable cadence.

- 1 video/week: strong
- 2 videos/week: stronger
- 1 video/2 weeks: acceptable but slower growth
- Random uploads: algorithm doesn't learn your pattern, distribution suffers

Quality beats quantity. One video per week that performs well beats three videos that get buried.
