# Titles & Thumbnails

Nobody watches a video they didn't click. Click-through rate (CTR) is the first gate. Everything else - watch time, session time, revenue - is downstream of whether someone clicked.

Titles and thumbnails work together. The thumbnail stops the scroll. The title seals the click.

---

## Titles

### The Combination Method (for topic selection)

Before writing a title, use the combination method to find a topic worth titling:

1. List 5–10 channels in your niche
2. Find their highest-view videos
3. Pick two topics that independently performed well
4. Combine them into one new concept

The combined topic gets a title. See [script-writing.md](./script-writing.md) for full combination method detail.

### Title Formulas

**The Contradiction:**
> "[Common belief] Is Wrong - Here's What Actually Works"
> "Why [Popular Advice] Kills Your [Desired Outcome]"

**The Specific Number:**
> "[X] [Things] That [Outcome] (Most People Miss #[N])"
> "I Tested [X] Methods for [Time Period] - Here's What Happened"

**The Stakes Frame:**
> "Do This Before [Event] or You'll [Negative Consequence]"
> "The [X]-Minute Fix That [Big Outcome]"

**The Insider:**
> "What [Industry/Experts] Won't Tell You About [Topic]"
> "The [Topic] Strategy Nobody Is Talking About"

**The Before/After:**
> "How I Went From [Bad State] to [Good State] in [Time]"
> "From [X] to [Y]: The Exact [Method] I Used"

### Title Rules

- **Under 60 characters** - longer gets cut off in search results
- **Front-load the value** - most important word in first 3 words
- **Use the audience's exact language** - mine this from Reddit comments and competitor video comment sections
- **One promise per title** - two promises = diluted click intent
- **Never clickbait beyond what you deliver** - YouTube tracks if people click and immediately leave. High CTR + low retention = suppressed video.

### Title Testing

Write 5 title options per video. Ask yourself: which one would I click if I didn't know what the video was about? Use that one. If you have access to YouTube Studio's A/B title test feature, use it.

## Thumbnails

### What a thumbnail must do

1. **Stop the scroll** - pattern interrupt against surrounding content
2. **Create curiosity** - make the next logical action "click to find out"
3. **Match the title** - they work as a unit, not independently

### Thumbnail Principles

**Faces work** (if you have them). Human faces with strong expressions drive higher CTR than any other visual. Curiosity, shock, excitement - all outperform neutral expressions.

**Contrast and simplicity.** Three elements maximum: background, subject, text. More than three = visual noise = no click.

**Large readable text.** Thumbnails display at 168×94px on mobile. If your text isn't readable at that size, it's invisible.

**Thumbnail-title unity.** The thumbnail poses the question. The title answers "why I should care." Or vice versa. They should feel like one complete thought together, not two separate messages.

### Thumbnail Text

- 3–5 words maximum
- Use the tension/curiosity angle from your title (not a repeat of the full title)
- High contrast: white or yellow text on dark background, or dark text on light background
- Never use thin fonts

### AI Thumbnail Tools

Tools like Tailsy generate thumbnail concepts from your title and topic. Use them for:
- Rapid first drafts
- Background generation
- Text overlay templates

Then customize: add your face (if applicable), adjust contrast, verify text reads at small size.

### Thumbnail Checklist

- [ ] Readable at 168×94px (mobile size)
- [ ] Maximum 3 visual elements
- [ ] Strong contrast between text and background
- [ ] Emotion or curiosity communicated in the image
- [ ] Title and thumbnail work together as one concept
- [ ] Tested: would you click this if scrolling past it?

## CTR Benchmarks

These are rough targets. Below minimum = title/thumbnail needs work.

| Context | Minimum | Good | Excellent |
|---------|---------|------|-----------|
| Browse features | 2% | 5% | 10%+ |
| Search | 5% | 10% | 20%+ |
| Suggested | 3% | 7% | 15%+ |

Check CTR in YouTube Studio → Reach tab → Impressions click-through rate. Filter by traffic source to see browse vs. search vs. suggested separately.

Low CTR on browse = thumbnail problem.
Low CTR on search = title problem.
Low CTR both = both need work, or topic has no audience.

## Common Mistakes

**Over-promising:** High CTR with viewers leaving in 30 seconds = YouTube suppresses the video. Deliver what the title and thumbnail promise.

**Too much text on thumbnail:** Treat thumbnail as a billboard at 60mph. One short punchy message only.

**Copying competitor thumbnails exactly:** Viewers see both in the same feed. Differentiate visually while using the same emotional triggers.

**Generic stock photo backgrounds:** They look cheap and don't stop scrolling. Use relevant, high-contrast images or solid color backgrounds with strong text instead.
